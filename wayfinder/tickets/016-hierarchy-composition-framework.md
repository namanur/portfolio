## Question

How do we control attention deliberately rather than decoratively? Investigate NN/g's visual-design research (scale, contrast, color, balance, grouping, consistency, grid alignment, white space) and Gestalt principles (proximity, similarity, figure-ground, closure, common grouping — informing how we group information instead of defaulting to cards). Establish a first-class **attention-management** framework: Primary → Secondary → Supporting → Background, with a per-section interrogation (what is the most important thing? what's second? what supports? what recedes? what is purely decorative and is any decorative element stealing attention?). Cover the full toolkit — scale, contrast, color, position, whitespace, grouping, typography, motion, density, image scale. Then a **composition/layout framework**: grid systems, asymmetry, alignment, negative space, rhythm, section transitions, content width, vertical spacing, overlap, cropping, edge alignment, full-bleed — documenting *when* each strategy is useful instead of defaulting to 12-column + centered max-width + cards. Core test to encode: "If everything is visually loud, nothing is actually important." Deliver a practical framework, every claim tagged Evidence / Convention / Taste / Project constraint.
